<h2 id="ch10-learn">What you'll learn</h2>

- How a monitor binds a requirement to evidence, and what MET, BREACHED and UNKNOWN(reason) each promise.
- How the release gate decides promotion monitors, on real runs, and how they differ from chance constraints.

<h2 id="ch10-monitor">A monitor binds evidence</h2>

The <Term id="contract-monitor">contract monitor</Term> evaluates requirements against evidence and reports MET, BREACHED or UNKNOWN.

TVL defines what each verdict promises. Production verdicts come from an <Term id="evaluator-profile">evaluator profile</Term> outside TVL, which must keep those promises; TVL's own tools decide only promotion monitors, from complete counts. A <Term id="monitor">monitor</Term> says which evidence checks one requirement, and how (<Ref to="rfc-0003" section="sections 3.2 and 4.5">RFC 0003</Ref>). Version 9 gives `slow_replies` two: `slow_replies_gate` judges the candidate once on the evaluation set (`phase: promotion`), and `slow_replies_prod` judges live traffic (`phase: runtime`):

<Example file="ch10/support-bot.tvl.yml" checker="tvl-validate" title="The support bot, version 9: the runtime monitor" lines="178-190" />

A runtime monitor judges <Term id="window">windows</Term>: back-to-back, never-overlapping ("tumbling") stretches of `tumbling_hours`, aligned to UTC midnight; evidence arriving within `grace_minutes` after a window ends still counts.

`confidence` is how sure the counts must make you before saying MET; `false_alarm_budget` is the chance you accept of even one false BREACHED in `horizon_days`; `min_samples` is the fewest units a rate verdict needs; `max_unresolved_fraction` is the largest share you tolerate as unclassifiable. This monitor is outside the frozen hosted profile. All five fields are required: leave one out, and the validator says `runtime_invalid_profile`. Promotion and zero-tolerance monitors have some defaults (<Ref to="rfc-0003" section="section 4.5">RFC 0003</Ref>). Version 9 also monitors refunds (zero tolerance), the recovery promise and liveness; escalation and refusal are stated but not monitored.

<h2 id="ch10-verdicts">Three verdicts, three promises</h2>

These steps describe rate bounds using `clopper_pearson_rational_v2`. Zero tolerance instead requires complete coverage for MET and an established violation for BREACHED. The hosted floating-point method has no claimed probability bound (<Ref to="semantics" section="section 12.6">formal semantics</Ref>).

1. You ensure that units go wrong independently, at one rate per window (the simple case of the general condition); that selection does not depend on whether a unit went wrong; and that the checker's labels are right.
2. **MET** says the evidence shows the requirement holds. If the bot is truly at or above `max_rate`, any one window is MET with probability at most 1 − `confidence`.
3. **BREACHED** says the evidence shows it is violated. If the bot is truly within `max_rate`, the chance of even one false BREACHED in `horizon_days` is at most `false_alarm_budget`.
4. **UNKNOWN(reason)** claims neither; a reason from a closed list says why (table below). Units that could not be classified never make a verdict stronger. **Absence of evidence is never MET.**

The budget is shared: 30 days of 6-hour windows are 120 windows, each with 1/120 of 0.05. With every unit classified, the exact method draws these lines. MET stops at 4 of 200, well under 5%, because MET must show the rate is below 5% with 95% confidence:

| Units in the window | BREACHED from | MET up to |
|---|---|---|
| 200 | 23 slow | 4 slow |
| 1,000 | 75 slow | 38 slow |

In between, UNKNOWN(inconclusive); more traffic narrows that band.

<Misconception wrong="Traffic is thin at night, so I set min_samples: 58.">
<p>Chapter 6: zero slow replies in 58 trials cannot show a 5% bound at 95% confidence. Larger windows can still establish MET and close an episode: `min_samples` is a lower bound, not a window-size limit. TVL requires MET to be reachable at the configured minimum, so version 9 fails validation at 58 (exit status 2, `runtime_met_unreachable_at_min_samples`: "Monitor cannot establish MET at decision.min_samples") and passes at 59. If traffic is too thin, lengthen the window.</p>
</Misconception>

<h2 id="ch10-zero">Zero tolerance needs complete coverage</h2>

One established violation makes a zero-tolerance window BREACHED, at any size. MET needs the window's population completely covered: every unit selected (no sampling), accounted for, classified and placed, every history complete, at least `min_samples` units, none violating. Otherwise the window is UNKNOWN with the applicable reason, including `insufficient` for too few units and `incomplete` for missing coverage. An empty window is UNKNOWN(`insufficient`), never MET: no traffic must not look like no violations (<Ref to="semantics" section="section 12.6">formal semantics</Ref>).

What an UNKNOWN tells you, and what you can change (three more, for late decisions, profile revisions and biased sampling, are in the formal semantics):

| Reason | What it means | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| `insufficient` | fewer units than `min_samples`, or none | lengthen the window |
| `inconclusive` | counts neither clearly good nor clearly bad | lengthen the window: more units |
| `unresolved` | too many units could not be classified | supply what the checker needs |
| `unplaceable` | too many units have no known start time | record every unit's start time |
| `incomplete` | zero tolerance: no violation, coverage incomplete | supply complete coverage, no sampling |
| `engine_required` | release gate: the unresolved count `u` or the unplaceable count `pl` is above 0 | resolve those units |

<h2 id="ch10-promotion">Promotion evidence, production evidence</h2>

A promotion monitor judges the candidate once, on the evaluation set. The evaluation harness writes its counts into the candidate's bundle under `monitor_outcomes`: `n` resolved units, `k` of them violating, and optionally `u` unresolved, `pl` unplaceable (start time unknown) and `complete`. Only the harness knows whether it ran the whole set, so zero tolerance needs `complete: true`; TVL never infers it (<Ref to="rfc-0003" section="section 11.4.2">RFC 0003</Ref>). Promotion and production verdicts are about separate populations: a promotion MET says nothing about next week, and a production BREACHED does not undo a promotion.

The gate joins promotion monitors with chapter 6's decision: BREACHED becomes Reject, UNKNOWN becomes NoDecision, MET passes, and any Reject wins (<Ref to="semantics" section="section 12.7">formal semantics</Ref>). Without promotion monitors it gates exactly as before. The public release gate does not interpret unresolved or unplaceable units, so it decides a monitor only when there are none; otherwise it returns UNKNOWN(`engine_required`), a NoDecision, even if a violation was counted.

<h2 id="ch10-chance">Chance constraint or promotion monitor?</h2>

In exact arithmetic, at matched confidence and with fully resolved counts, a chance-constraint pass is exactly a rate monitor's MET test on the same counts. The public chance constraint computes its bound in floating point, so the implementations can disagree at exact ties (<Ref to="semantics" section="section 12.7">formal semantics</Ref>). They differ in failure. The chance constraint says "show that it holds, or Reject"; the monitor says "show that it holds, show that it is broken, or say UNKNOWN", and only a shown breach rejects.

Chapter 6's 50-ticket candidate (`ch10/candidate-50.synthetic.measure.yml`) shows the difference. Zero slow replies in 50 sounds good, but 50 tickets are too few to show a 5% bound at 95% confidence (that takes 59), and they show no breach either. So the gate's `--json` report gives `slow_replies_gate` UNKNOWN(inconclusive), which on its own would be NoDecision, while the chance constraint, not shown to hold, says Reject. Any Reject wins: the decision is `Reject`.

**Two methods.** `clopper_pearson_exact_v1`, only for the frozen hosted latency profile `backend_v1`, runs the rational method's tests in floating point, with no claimed probability bound; near a tie the two can disagree (<Ref to="semantics" section="sections 12.6 and 12.14">formal semantics</Ref>).

<KeyTakeaways>
  <ul>
    <li>Exact-rational rate verdicts carry stated error bounds under a stated model. Zero-tolerance verdicts carry coverage and violation claims. UNKNOWN explains why neither applicable claim was established.</li>
    <li>No evidence is never MET. Zero-tolerance promotion needs `complete: true`.</li>
    <li>The release gate decides promotion monitors only without unresolved or unplaceable units; otherwise UNKNOWN(engine_required), a NoDecision. Any Reject wins.</li>
  </ul>
</KeyTakeaways>
