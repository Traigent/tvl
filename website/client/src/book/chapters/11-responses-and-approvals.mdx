<h2 id="ch11-learn">What you'll learn</h2>

- The response ladder; the approval levels `none`, `standing` and `human`; why a suspension needs a person and stays after recovery.
- When an optimization candidate may be deployed, and why deploying it never proves recovery.

<h2 id="ch11-ladder">The ladder</h2>

On a slow-reply breach the team wants to warn, open a next step, roll back after six hours, and stop taking tickets if recovery is overdue. A response is a named list of rules, each with an `id`, a `trigger` (a monitor and one of its events) and one action, `do`, from a sealed ladder ordered by impact on production (<Ref to="semantics" section="section 12.10">formal semantics</Ref>):

The actions mean: `notify`, an alert with a `severity`; `open_next_step`, a suggested next step for the maintainers; `optimize`, a bounded optimization that produces a candidate; `rollback`, a return to a certified configuration that is eligible then; `throttle`, admissions cut to `rate_floor` (a fraction) of normal; `degrade`, new work sent to a certified fallback; `suspend`, no new work, existing work finished under `static_unavailable` or `route_to_human`. The guards `block_action` and `halt_unit` suppress the violating event, or stop the unit, before the effect.

<h2 id="ch11-approval">Who may decide</h2>

- **`none`**, for `notify`, `open_next_step` and the guards: the contract itself permits the narrowly specified action without an additional standing grant or human approval; its other conditions still apply.
- **`standing`**, for `optimize`, `rollback`, `throttle` and `degrade`: a <Term id="standing-grant">standing grant</Term>, owner-signed, outside TVL, limited in uses (and cost, for `optimize`), expiring within 30 days.
- **`human`**, for the same four, and always for `suspend`: an authorized person approves each occurrence.

A module cannot name an approver or contain a grant (<Ref to="rfc-0003" section="section 7.3">RFC 0003</Ref>), and a `rollback` with `approval: none` is rejected (`response_irreversible_without_approval`).

**Declaring is not permitting, and permitting is not executing.** A response declares permitted reactions and required authority. Grants and human approvals live outside the module. Evaluating production evidence and carrying out permitted actions belong to an evaluator profile and platform outside TVL. Declaring, validating or compiling a response performs no action (<Ref to="semantics" section="sections 12.10 and 12.11">formal semantics</Ref>).

<h2 id="ch11-optimize">Optimize: a candidate, then a separate deploy</h2>

An `optimize` rule may set `deploy: never` or `deploy: if_promoted` (<Ref to="rfc-0003" section="section 7.6">RFC 0003</Ref>). The default, `never`, stops at a candidate. Deploying it outside this response is an ordinary promotion.

With `if_promoted`, deployment is a separate effect. The rule's `approval` applies to each effect on its own. Under `human`, a person approves the optimization and the deploy separately. A standing grant must name the deploy effect to cover it; a grant for optimization alone leaves the deploy needing a human.

The triggering monitor's requirement must also have a promotion monitor (`response_deploy_without_promotion_monitor`). The candidate must come from this rule in this episode and earn Promote from the joined gate under the current contract, including every promotion monitor. Reject and NoDecision never deploy.

At deployment, the contract and authority must still be current, the episode still open, and the deployed configuration unchanged from the recorded precondition. A configuration that breached in an open episode is ineligible.

`deploy: if_promoted` is allowed only on `episode_opened`, `episode_continued` and `episode_open_for`. Recovery, burn, liveness and unit triggers cannot use it.

If authority or the gate record cannot be reached, the deploy waits. A refused or failed deploy changes nothing in production and is not retried with another candidate. No rollback follows automatically.

A deploy never closes a breach or satisfies a recovery promise. Its promotion evidence is about the evaluation set. The episode stays open until enough consecutive MET production windows resolve it, or it is withdrawn; withdrawal is not recovery.

<h2 id="ch11-suspend">Suspend: a person, and no automatic return</h2>

Suspend, the top rung, needs `approval: human` per episode, even under a standing grant, and an event showing the trouble persisted: `episode_continued` (a further BREACHED window), `episode_open_for` or `recovery_overdue`, never `episode_opened` alone. It is latched: once the episode resolves or is withdrawn, a person may resume; nothing else lifts it (<Ref to="semantics" section="section 12.11">formal semantics</Ref>). Suspension stops new admissions and drains existing work; any window containing no units is UNKNOWN(insufficient), never MET: empty traffic cannot establish recovery or lift the suspension.

<Misconception wrong="When the episode resolves, a throttle or rollback undoes itself.">
<p>A throttle or degrade stays until an authorized person releases it. A rollback is a repair, not proof of recovery; undoing it is a new promotion (<Ref to="semantics" section="section 12.11">formal semantics</Ref>).</p>
</Misconception>

<KeyTakeaways>
  <ul>
    <li>One monitor event, one action from a sealed ladder. Repairs need a standing grant at least; suspend needs a person, and only a human resume lifts it. Declaring is not permitting.</li>
    <li>An optimization and its deploy need separate authority. Only a promoted candidate may deploy; only production evidence can establish recovery.</li>
  </ul>
</KeyTakeaways>
